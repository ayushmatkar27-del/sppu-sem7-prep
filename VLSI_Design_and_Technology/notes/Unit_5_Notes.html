💡 In Simple Words: What is a Stick Diagram? (The Metro Map Analogy)
Think of a Stick Diagram like a city Metro/Subway map. A Metro map does not care about exact kilometer distances or tunnel widths—it only uses colored lines to show which lines cross each other and where the stations (contacts) are.
Golden Rule: Wherever a Red (Polysilicon) line crosses a Green (n-Diffusion) or Yellow (p-Diffusion) line, a MOSFET transistor is born!

🎯 1.5 Euler's Path Method & 18-Mark Half Adder Design

💡 In Simple Words: What is Euler's Path?
Imagine drawing a shape without lifting your pen. In VLSI, lifting your pen means breaking the silicon diffusion strip into disconnected islands, which wastes chip space. Euler's Path finds a single left-to-right order of inputs ($A o C o B$) that visits every transistor once in both PMOS and NMOS graphs, keeping diffusion strips 100% continuous!
  • Half Adder Equations (Asked in Nov 2022 — 18 Marks): $ ext{Carry} = A \cdot B = \overline{\overline{A \cdot B}}$ (2-input NAND + Inverter = 6 transistors); $ ext{Sum} = A \oplus B = (A+B)\cdot \overline{AB}$.
  • Common Euler Sequence: For Carry: $A - B$. For Sum: $A - B - ar{A} - ar{B}$. Place vertical Polysilicon tracks in this order across unbroken p-diffusion and n-diffusion strips.

🎯 2.1 Antenna Effect (Plasma-Induced Gate Oxide Breakdown)

💡 In Simple Words (The Lightning Rod Analogy)
Think of a long metal wire on a chip as a TV antenna or lightning rod. During manufacturing, plasma etching shoots charged ions onto the wafer.
1. A long floating wire acts like an antenna and collects static charge.
2. If it connects only to a transistor's thin gate ($ ext{SiO}_2$) with no path to ground, high voltage builds up ($V = Q/C$) and burns a hole through the gate insulation, destroying the transistor!
Fig 2.1: Antenna Effect Gate Breakdown & Remedies (Metal Hopping + Antenna Diode)
$$ ext{Antenna Ratio} = rac{ ext{Area of Metal Interconnect}}{ ext{Area of Connected Gate Oxide}} \le ext{Safe Limit (typically } 200 ext{–}500 ext{)}$$

🎯 2.2 Electromigration (EM) & Black's Equation

💡 In Simple Words (River Pushing Pebbles Analogy)
Imagine a fast-flowing river pushing sand and pebbles downstream. Inside a wire, high-speed flowing electrons act like an "electron wind" that bumps into metal atoms and physically pushes them forward. Over time, empty holes (Voids $ o$ open circuit) form at the start, and piles (Hillocks $ o$ short circuit) form at the end!
Fig 2.2: Electromigration Voids & Hillocks
Black's Equation (MTTF): $$ ext{MTTF} = rac{A}{J^n} \exp\left( rac{E_a}{kT} ight)$$
• Fixes: Widen $V_{DD}/GND$ rails (lowers $J$), use Copper (Cu) instead of Al, and add redundant multiple vias.

🎯 2.3 Interconnect Crosstalk

💡 In Simple Words (Highway Lanes Analogy)
Two parallel metal wires sitting very close together form a tiny capacitor ($C_c$). When one wire switches rapidly from $0 o 1$ (Aggressor Line), it pushes a fake voltage spike onto its quiet neighbor (Victim Line)—like a speeding truck shaking a small car in the next lane!
$$\Delta V_{ ext{victim}} = V_{ ext{swing}} \cdot \left( rac{C_c}{C_c + C_{ ext{gnd}}} ight) \qquad ext{• Prevention: GND Shielding, Larger Spacing, Orthogonal Metal Layers}$$

🏆 Topic 3: Lambda ($\lambda$) Based Layout Design Rules (Mead-Conway)

Asked 6 times (Nov 2022, May 2023, Nov 2023, May 2024, Nov 2024, May 2025)
💡 In Simple Words (The LEGO Brick Analogy)
Imagine building a castle with LEGO bricks where every wall is "2 studs wide" and "3 studs apart." If a factory makes LEGO bricks half as big, you don't redesign the castle—it shrinks automatically! $\lambda$ (Lambda) is that single "stud unit" (half of minimum transistor length, $L_{\min}=2\lambda$). All layout sizes scale effortlessly across technologies simply by changing $\lambda$.

🏆 Topic 4: SPICE Simulation & CMOS Inverter Code (AC, DC, Transient)

Asked 6 times (Nov 2023, Nov 2024, May 2025, Nov 2025, May 2026)
💡 In Simple Words: Virtual Breadboard
Making a real chip costs millions of dollars. SPICE is a virtual breadboard & oscilloscope where you describe your circuit in text (Netlist) to test voltages, delays, and frequencies before manufacturing.
  • DC Analysis (.DC): Sweeps $V_{ ext{in}}$ from $0 o V_{DD}$ to plot the VTC curve and find Noise Margins ($NM_H, NM_L$).
  • Transient Analysis (.TRAN): Applies a pulse over time to measure rise time ($t_r$), fall time ($t_f$), and propagation delay ($t_{pd}$).
  • AC Analysis (.AC): Sweeps frequency to plot Bode Magnitude (dB) & Phase and find 3-dB bandwidth.
* SPICE Netlist: CMOS Inverter AC Analysis (Nodes: 1=VDD, 2=Vin, 3=Vout, 0=GND)
VDD  1  0  DC 1.8V
VIN  2  0  DC 0.9V  AC 1.0V                  ; DC Bias at 0.9V + 1V AC Sweep

M1   3  2  1  1  PMOS_MOD  W=2.0u  L=0.18u   ; PMOS Pull-Up (Source & Bulk to VDD)
M2   3  2  0  0  NMOS_MOD  W=1.0u  L=0.18u   ; NMOS Pull-Down (Source & Bulk to GND)

.MODEL NMOS_MOD NMOS (LEVEL=1 VTO=0.4 KP=120u)
.MODEL PMOS_MOD PMOS (LEVEL=1 VTO=-0.4 KP=40u)

.AC DEC 10 1K 10G                            ; Decade sweep: 1 kHz to 10 GHz
.PRINT AC VDB(3) VP(3)                       ; Output Magnitude (dB) & Phase
