🏆 Question 4: Short-Channel Non-Ideal Effects

Marks: 6 to 8 Marks | Frequency: 11x across 9 papers (100% Guaranteed)

  • Velocity Saturation: High lateral electric field causes drift velocity to saturate at $v_{ ext{sat}} pprox 10^7 ext{ cm/s}$, collapsing quadratic saturation current to a linear relation: I_D,sat = W · Cox · vsat · (Vgs - Vth).
  • Hot Carrier Injection (HCI): High kinetic energy electrons near the drain punch through the Si-SiO2 barrier, causing permanent trapped oxide charges and threshold voltage drift.
  • Drain-Induced Barrier Lowering (DIBL): High drain bias lowers the source-to-channel potential barrier, leading to threshold reduction: ΔVth = -η · Vds and exponential subthreshold leakage.
  • Channel Length Modulation (CLM): Pinch-off point moves inward, reducing effective channel length $L' = L - \Delta L$ and creating finite output resistance $r_o = 1/(\lambda I_D)$.
  • Body Effect: Reverse substrate bias $V_{SB} > 0$ widens channel depletion layer: Vth = Vth0 + γ(√(2φF + Vsb) - √(2φF)).

🏆 Question 5: CMOS Power Dissipation & Solved University Numerical

Marks: 6 to 8 Marks | Frequency: 4x across 3 papers (50% Priority)

📝 Solved University Numerical Problem:
"A CMOS logic circuit operates at 10 MHz and 3 V with a load of 100 pF. The static power is 100 μW. Calculate total power dissipation if frequency increases to 100 MHz."

Step 1: Dynamic Power at 10 MHz:
P_dyn1 = C_L · Vdd^2 · f1 = (100 × 10^-12) × 3^2 × (10 × 10^6) = 9.0 mW
P_total1 = 9.0 mW + 0.1 mW = 9.1 mW

Step 2: Dynamic Power at 100 MHz:
P_dyn2 = C_L · Vdd^2 · f2 = (100 × 10^-12) × 3^2 × (100 × 10^6) = 90.0 mW
P_total2 = 90.0 mW + 0.1 mW = 90.1 mW

🏆 Question 8: Latch-Up Effect & SCR Prevention in CMOS

Marks: 6 to 8 Marks | Frequency: 3x across 3 papers (40% Priority)

Latch-up is caused by parasitic bipolar transistors forming a regenerative 4-layer $p ext{-}n ext{-}p ext{-}n$ Silicon Controlled Rectifier (SCR) path between $V_{DD}$ and $GND$. When loop gain $eta_1 \cdot eta_2 \ge 1$, massive short-circuit currents melt the chip.

  • Prevention 1 (Guard Rings): Surround n-wells with $p^+$ rings tied to $GND$, and NMOS with $n^+$ rings tied to $V_{DD}$.
  • Prevention 2 (Well Taps): Insert substrate/well contacts every $20\,\mu ext{m}$ to minimize parasitic resistances.
  • Prevention 3 (Epitaxial Substrate): Use heavily doped low-resistivity substrates with thin epitaxial layers.
