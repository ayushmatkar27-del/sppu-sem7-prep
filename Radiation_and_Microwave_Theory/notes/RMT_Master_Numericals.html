📌 Problem 1.1: Symmetric Directional Coupler with Load & Two Bolometers

🔥 Repeated 4x (2022-2026) 6 Marks
SPPU University Examination Question:
"A symmetric directional coupler with infinite directivity and a forward attenuation of $20\text{ dB}$ is used to monitor the power delivered to a load $Z_L$. Bolometer 1 introduces a $\text{VSWR} = 2.0$ on arm 4; bolometer 2 is matched to arm 3. If bolometer 1 reads $8\text{ mW}$ and bolometer 2 reads $2\text{ mW}$, find:
(i) The amount of power dissipated in the load $Z_L$;
(ii) The VSWR on arm 2."
Port 1 (Source Input P_1) ────────────────────────► Port 2 (Load Z_L, P_L) ││ (20 dB Coupling) Port 3 (Reverse Sampling P_3) ◄──────────────────── Port 4 (Forward Sampling P_4) (Bolometer 2, Matched VSWR=1) (Bolometer 1, Mismatched VSWR=2)
📋 1. Given Parameters & Operating Conditions
Directivity ($D$): $D = \infty$ (Ideal coupler, zero leakage from Port 1 to Port 3)
Forward Attenuation ($C$): $C = 20\text{ dB}$ ($10^{20/10} = 100$ linear power ratio)
Bolometer 1 Reading ($P_{b1}$): $P_{b1} = 8\text{ mW}$ (Power absorbed at Port 4)
Arm 4 VSWR (Bolometer 1): $\text{VSWR}_4 = 2.0$ (Mismatched detector)
Bolometer 2 Reading ($P_{b2}$): $P_{b2} = 2\text{ mW}$ (Power absorbed at Port 3)
Arm 3 VSWR (Bolometer 2): $\text{VSWR}_3 = 1.0$ ($\Gamma_3 = 0$, perfectly matched)
📐 2. Key Governing Mathematical Formulas
  1. Reflection Coefficient from VSWR:
    $$\Gamma = \frac{\text{VSWR} - 1}{\text{VSWR} + 1}$$
  2. Incident Power on Mismatched Bolometer:
    $$P_{\text{absorbed}} = P_{\text{inc}} \cdot (1 - |\Gamma|^2) \implies P_{\text{inc}} = \frac{P_{\text{absorbed}}}{1 - |\Gamma|^2}$$
  3. Coupling Power Ratios ($20\text{ dB}$):
    $$\frac{P_{\text{forward inc}}}{P_{4,\text{inc}}} = 10^{C/10} = 100, \quad \frac{P_{\text{reflected}}}{P_{3,\text{inc}}} = 10^{C/10} = 100$$
  4. Power Dissipated in Load:
    $$P_L = P_{\text{forward inc}} - P_{\text{reflected}}$$
  5. Load VSWR on Arm 2:
    $$|\Gamma_L| = \sqrt{\frac{P_{\text{reflected}}}{P_{\text{forward inc}}}}, \quad \text{VSWR}_2 = \frac{1 + |\Gamma_L|}{1 - |\Gamma_L|}$$
⚡ 3. Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation
🔹 Step 1: Calculate Incident Power on Forward Arm 4 ($P_{4,\text{inc}}$)

Bolometer 1 introduces a $\text{VSWR} = 2.0$. Finding reflection coefficient $\Gamma_4$ and transmission factor:

$$\begin{aligned} \Gamma_4 &= \frac{\text{VSWR}_4 - 1}{\text{VSWR}_4 + 1} = \frac{2.0 - 1}{2.0 + 1} = \frac{1}{3} \approx 0.3333 \\[6pt] 1 - |\Gamma_4|^2 &= 1 - \left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^2 = 1 - \frac{1}{9} = \frac{8}{9} \approx 0.8889 \\[6pt] P_{4,\text{inc}} &= \frac{P_{b1}}{1 - |\Gamma_4|^2} = \frac{8\text{ mW}}{\frac{8}{9}} = 8 \times \frac{9}{8} = \mathbf{9.0\text{ mW}} \end{aligned}$$
🔹 Step 2: Calculate Forward Incident Power Delivered toward Load ($P_{\text{forward inc}}$)

Since forward attenuation is $20\text{ dB}$, the main line power is 100 times the coupled power:

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{P_{\text{forward inc}}}{P_{4,\text{inc}}} &= 10^{20/10} = 100 \\[6pt] P_{\text{forward inc}} &= 100 \times P_{4,\text{inc}} = 100 \times 9.0\text{ mW} = \mathbf{900\text{ mW}} \quad (0.90\text{ W}) \end{aligned}$$
🔹 Step 3: Calculate Reflected Power from Load ($P_{\text{reflected}}$)

Bolometer 2 is matched ($\text{VSWR}_3 = 1.0 \implies \Gamma_3 = 0$), so it absorbs 100% of power incident on Arm 3 ($P_{3,\text{inc}} = P_{b2} = 2\text{ mW}$):

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{P_{\text{reflected}}}{P_{3,\text{inc}}} &= 10^{20/10} = 100 \\[6pt] P_{\text{reflected}} &= 100 \times P_{3,\text{inc}} = 100 \times 2.0\text{ mW} = \mathbf{200\text{ mW}} \quad (0.20\text{ W}) \end{aligned}$$
🔹 Step 4: Calculate Net Power Dissipated in the Load $Z_L$
$$\begin{aligned} P_L &= P_{\text{forward inc}} - P_{\text{reflected}} \\[6pt] P_L &= 900\text{ mW} - 200\text{ mW} = \mathbf{700\text{ mW}} \quad (0.70\text{ W}) \end{aligned}$$
🔹 Step 5: Calculate Reflection Coefficient & VSWR on Arm 2 (Load Arm)
$$\begin{aligned} |\Gamma_L|^2 &= \frac{P_{\text{reflected}}}{P_{\text{forward inc}}} = \frac{200\text{ mW}}{900\text{ mW}} = \frac{2}{9} \approx 0.2222 \\[6pt] |\Gamma_L| &= \sqrt{\frac{2}{9}} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{3} = \frac{1.4142}{3} \approx \mathbf{0.4714} \\[6pt] \text{VSWR}_2 &= \frac{1 + |\Gamma_L|}{1 - |\Gamma_L|} = \frac{1 + 0.4714}{1 - 0.4714} = \frac{1.4714}{0.5286} \approx \mathbf{2.783} \end{aligned}$$
🏆 4. Final Boxed Examination Results
Part (i) Amount of Power Dissipated in Load $Z_L$: $P_L = 700 ext{ mW} = 0.70 ext{ W}$
Part (ii) Voltage Standing Wave Ratio (VSWR) on Arm 2: $ ext{VSWR}_2 = 2.78$

📌 Problem 1.2: Output Powers of a Lossless Directional Coupler

🔥 Repeated 3x (2024-2026) 6 Marks
SPPU University Examination Question:
"A $10\text{ dB}$ directional coupler has a directivity of $40\text{ dB}$. If the applied input power $P_1 = 10\text{ mW}$, calculate the power outputs at port 2 (through), port 3 (isolated), and port 4 (coupled) of a lossless directional coupler."
📋 1. Given Parameters
Input Power ($P_1$): $P_1 = 10\text{ mW}$
Coupling Factor ($C$): $C = 10\text{ dB}$
Directivity ($D$): $D = 40\text{ dB}$
Loss Condition: Lossless Coupler ($P_1 = P_2 + P_3 + P_4$)
📐 2. Key Governing Mathematical Formulas
  1. Coupling Factor ($C$ in dB):
    $$C = 10\log_{10}\left(\frac{P_1}{P_4}\right) \implies \frac{P_1}{P_4} = 10^{C/10} \implies P_4 = \frac{P_1}{10^{C/10}}$$
  2. Directivity ($D$ in dB):
    $$D = 10\log_{10}\left(\frac{P_4}{P_3}\right) \implies \frac{P_4}{P_3} = 10^{D/10} \implies P_3 = \frac{P_4}{10^{D/10}}$$
  3. Conservation of Power (Lossless Junction):
    $$P_2 = P_1 - P_4 - P_3$$
⚡ 3. Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation
🔹 Step 1: Calculate Coupled Port Power ($P_4$)
$$\begin{aligned} C &= 10 = 10\log_{10}\left(\frac{10\text{ mW}}{P_4}\right) \\[6pt] \frac{10\text{ mW}}{P_4} &= 10^{10/10} = 10^1 = 10 \\[6pt] P_4 &= \frac{10\text{ mW}}{10} = \mathbf{1.0\text{ mW}} \end{aligned}$$
🔹 Step 2: Calculate Isolated Port Power ($P_3$)
$$\begin{aligned} D &= 40 = 10\log_{10}\left(\frac{P_4}{P_3}\right) \\[6pt] \frac{P_4}{P_3} &= 10^{40/10} = 10^4 = 10,000 \\[6pt] P_3 &= \frac{P_4}{10,000} = \frac{1.0\text{ mW}}{10,000} = 10^{-4}\text{ mW} = \mathbf{0.1\ \mu\text{W}} \quad (100\text{ nW}) \end{aligned}$$
🔹 Step 3: Calculate Through Port Power ($P_2$)
$$\begin{aligned} P_2 &= P_1 - P_4 - P_3 \\[6pt] P_2 &= 10\text{ mW} - 1.0\text{ mW} - 0.0001\text{ mW} = \mathbf{8.9999\text{ mW}} \approx \mathbf{9.0\text{ mW}} \end{aligned}$$
🏆 4. Final Boxed Examination Results
Port 2 Through Port Output Power: $P_2 = 8.9999\text{ mW} \approx 9.0\text{ mW}$
Port 3 Isolated Port Output Power: $P_3 = 0.1\ \mu\text{W} = 100\text{ nW}$
Port 4 Coupled Port Output Power: $P_4 = 1.0\text{ mW}$

📌 Problem 1.3: Directional Coupler with Insertion Loss

Nov/Dec 2024, Q1(a) 6 Marks
SPPU University Examination Question:
"A $2\text{ Watt}$ power source is connected to the input of a directional coupler with $C = 20\text{ dB}$, $D = 25\text{ dB}$, and an insertion loss of $0.7\text{ dB}$. Find the output power at the through, coupled, and isolated ports. Assume all ports to be matched."
📋 1. Given Parameters
Input Power ($P_1$):$P_1 = 2\text{ W} = 2000\text{ mW}$
Coupling Factor ($C$):$C = 20\text{ dB}$
Directivity ($D$):$D = 25\text{ dB}$
Insertion Loss ($\text{IL}$):$\text{IL} = 0.7\text{ dB}$
⚡ 3. Mathematical Calculations
🔹 Step 1: Calculate Coupled Power ($P_4$)
$$\frac{P_1}{P_4} = 10^{20/10} = 100 \implies P_4 = \frac{2000\text{ mW}}{100} = \mathbf{20.0\text{ mW}} \quad (0.02\text{ W})$$
🔹 Step 2: Calculate Isolated Power ($P_3$)
$$\frac{P_4}{P_3} = 10^{25/10} = 10^{2.5} \approx 316.228 \implies P_3 = \frac{20\text{ mW}}{316.228} = \mathbf{0.0632\text{ mW}} = \mathbf{63.2\ \mu\text{W}}$$
🔹 Step 3: Calculate Through Power ($P_2$) from Insertion Loss
$$\text{IL} = 10\log_{10}\left(\frac{P_1}{P_2}\right) = 0.7\text{ dB} \implies \frac{P_1}{P_2} = 10^{0.07} \approx 1.1749$$ $$P_2 = \frac{2000\text{ mW}}{1.1749} = \mathbf{1702.28\text{ mW}} \approx \mathbf{1.702\text{ W}}$$
🏆 4. Final Boxed Results
P2 (Through)$P_2 = 1.702\text{ W}$
P4 (Coupled)$P_4 = 20.0\text{ mW}$
P3 (Isolated)$P_3 = 63.2\ \mu\text{W}$

📌 Problem 1.4: VSWR Measurement using Two Identical Directional Couplers

Nov/Dec 2024, Q8(c) 4 Marks
SPPU University Examination Question:
"Two identical directional couplers are used in a waveguide to sample the incident and reflected powers. The output of the two couplers are found to be $2.5\text{ mW}$ and $0.25\text{ mW}$. Determine the VSWR in the waveguide."
📋 1. Given Parameters
Sampled Forward Power ($P_f$):$P_f = 2.5\text{ mW}$
Sampled Reverse Power ($P_r$):$P_r = 0.25\text{ mW}$
⚡ 3. Mathematical Calculations
🔹 Step 1: Calculate Power Reflection Ratio & Reflection Coefficient ($|\Gamma|$)
$$\begin{aligned} \frac{P_{\text{reflected}}}{P_{\text{incident}}} &= \frac{P_r}{P_f} = \frac{0.25\text{ mW}}{2.5\text{ mW}} = 0.10 \\[6pt] |\Gamma| &= \sqrt{\frac{P_r}{P_f}} = \sqrt{0.10} \approx \mathbf{0.3162} \end{aligned}$$
🔹 Step 2: Calculate Voltage Standing Wave Ratio (VSWR)
$$\text{VSWR} = \frac{1 + |\Gamma|}{1 - |\Gamma|} = \frac{1 + 0.3162}{1 - 0.3162} = \frac{1.3162}{0.6838} \approx \mathbf{1.925}$$
🏆 4. Final Boxed Result
VSWR$\text{VSWR} = 1.925$

📌 Problem 2.1: Scattering Matrix of a Ferrite Isolator

🔥 Repeated 3x (2023-2024) 6 Marks
SPPU University Examination Question:
"An Isolator has an insertion loss of $0.5\text{ dB}$ and an isolation of $30\text{ dB}$. Determine the scattering matrix of the isolator if the isolated ports are perfectly matched to the junction."
📋 1. Given Parameters
Insertion Loss ($\text{IL}$):$\text{IL} = 0.5\text{ dB}$
Isolation ($\text{Iso}$):$\text{Iso} = 30\text{ dB}$
Matched Ports:$S_{11} = S_{22} = 0$
📐 2. Governing Formulas
$$\text{IL (dB)} = -20\log_{10}|S_{21}| \implies |S_{21}| = 10^{-\text{IL}/20}, \quad \text{Iso (dB)} = -20\log_{10}|S_{12}| \implies |S_{12}| = 10^{-\text{Iso}/20}$$
⚡ 3. Mathematical Calculations
🔹 Step 1: Forward Transmission Coefficient ($|S_{21}|$)
$$|S_{21}| = 10^{-0.5 / 20} = 10^{-0.025} \approx \mathbf{0.9441}$$
🔹 Step 2: Reverse Leakage Coefficient ($|S_{12}|$)
$$|S_{12}| = 10^{-30 / 20} = 10^{-1.5} \approx \mathbf{0.03162}$$
🏆 4. Final [S]-Matrix Result
[S] Matrix $$[S] = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0.0316 \\ 0.9441 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

📌 Problem 2.2: Scattering Matrix of a 3-Port Ferrite Circulator

May/Jun 2025, Q1(c) 6 Marks
SPPU University Examination Question:
"Determine the $[S]$ matrix of a 3-port circulator. Given insertion loss of $0.5\text{ dB}$, isolation of $20\text{ dB}$, and $\text{VSWR} = 2$ at all three ports."
📋 1. Given Parameters
Insertion Loss ($\text{IL}$):$\text{IL} = 0.5\text{ dB}$ (Forward paths $1\to 2, 2\to 3, 3\to 1$)
Isolation ($\text{Iso}$):$\text{Iso} = 20\text{ dB}$ (Reverse paths $2\to 1, 3\to 2, 1\to 3$)
Port VSWR:$\text{VSWR} = 2.0$ at all ports
⚡ 3. Mathematical Calculations
🔹 Step 1: Reflection Coefficients ($S_{11} = S_{22} = S_{33}$)
$$|S_{ii}| = \frac{\text{VSWR} - 1}{\text{VSWR} + 1} = \frac{2.0 - 1}{2.0 + 1} = \frac{1}{3} \approx \mathbf{0.3333}$$
🔹 Step 2: Forward Transmission ($S_{21} = S_{32} = S_{13}$)
$$|S_{21}| = 10^{-0.5 / 20} = 10^{-0.025} \approx \mathbf{0.9441}$$
🔹 Step 3: Reverse Leakage ($S_{12} = S_{23} = S_{31}$)
$$|S_{12}| = 10^{-20 / 20} = 10^{-1.0} = \mathbf{0.1000}$$
🏆 4. Final [S]-Matrix Result
[S] Matrix $$[S] = \begin{bmatrix} 0.3333 & 0.1000 & 0.9441 \\ 0.9441 & 0.3333 & 0.1000 \\ 0.1000 & 0.9441 & 0.3333 \end{bmatrix}$$

📌 Problem 3.1: Power Distribution in a Lossless H-Plane Tee

May/Jun 2024, Q1(c) 6 Marks
SPPU University Examination Question:
"A signal of power $40\text{ mW}$ is fed into the collinear ports of a lossless H-Plane tee. Determine the powers in the remaining ports when other ports are terminated with matched impedance."
⚡ 3. Mathematical Derivations
🔹 Case I: Input Fed into Port 3 (H-Arm, $P_3 = 40\text{ mW}$)

In an H-Plane Tee, an input to Port 3 divides equally and in-phase into collinear ports 1 and 2 with zero reflection back into Port 3 ($S_{33} = 0$):

$$P_1 = \frac{1}{2} P_3 = \frac{40\text{ mW}}{2} = \mathbf{20.0\text{ mW}}, \quad P_2 = \frac{1}{2} P_3 = \frac{40\text{ mW}}{2} = \mathbf{20.0\text{ mW}}$$
🔹 Case II: Input Fed into Collinear Port 1 ($P_1 = 40\text{ mW}$)

Using the standard scattering matrix $[S]$ for H-Plane Tee where $S_{11} = 1/2, S_{21} = 1/2, S_{31} = 1/\sqrt{2}$:

$$\begin{aligned} P_{1,\text{reflected}} &= |S_{11}|^2 P_1 = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 (40\text{ mW}) = \mathbf{10.0\text{ mW}} \\[6pt] P_{2,\text{transmitted}} &= |S_{21}|^2 P_1 = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 (40\text{ mW}) = \mathbf{10.0\text{ mW}} \\[6pt] P_{3,\text{coupled}} &= |S_{31}|^2 P_1 = \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^2 (40\text{ mW}) = \mathbf{20.0\text{ mW}} \end{aligned}$$
🏆 4. Final Power Distribution Summary
Port 3 Fed$P_1 = 20\text{ mW}, \quad P_2 = 20\text{ mW}$
Port 1 Fed$P_1 = 10\text{ mW}, \quad P_2 = 10\text{ mW}, \quad P_3 = 20\text{ mW}$

📌 Problem 4.1: DC Input Power, RF Output Power, and Electronic Efficiency

🔥 Repeated 3x (2025-2026) 6 Marks
SPPU University Examination Question:
"A reflex klystron operates at the peak mode of $n = 2$ ($1\frac{3}{4}$ mode) with beam voltage $V_0 = 300\text{ V}$, beam current $I_0 = 20\text{ mA}$, and RF signal voltage $V_1 = 40\text{ V}$. Determine:
(i) The input power;
(ii) The output power;
(iii) The electronic efficiency."
📋 1. Given Parameters
Beam DC Voltage ($V_0$):$V_0 = 300\text{ V}$
Beam DC Current ($I_0$):$I_0 = 20\text{ mA} = 0.020\text{ A}$
RF Signal Voltage ($V_1$):$V_1 = 40\text{ V}$
Mode Number ($n$):$n = 2 \implies N = 1\frac{3}{4} = 1.75$
Coupling Factor ($\beta_i$):$\beta_i \approx 1.0$
📐 2. Key Governing Mathematical Formulas
  1. DC Input Power:
    $$P_{\text{in}} = V_0 \times I_0$$
  2. Round-Trip Transit Angle:
    $$\theta_0 = 2\pi\left(n - \frac{1}{4}\right) = 2\pi(1.75) = 3.5\pi \approx 10.9956\text{ rad}$$
  3. Bunching Parameter:
    $$X' = \frac{\beta_i V_1}{2V_0} \theta_0$$
  4. RF Output Power:
    $$P_{\text{out}} = \frac{2 V_0 I_0 X' J_1(X')}{\theta_0} = V_1 I_0 J_1(X')$$
  5. Electronic Efficiency:
    $$\eta = \frac{P_{\text{out}}}{P_{\text{in}}} \times 100\%$$
⚡ 3. Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation
🔹 Step 1: Calculate DC Input Power ($P_{\text{in}}$)
$$P_{\text{in}} = V_0 I_0 = 300\text{ V} \times 0.020\text{ A} = \mathbf{6.0\text{ W}} \quad (6000\text{ mW})$$
🔹 Step 2: Calculate Bunching Parameter ($X'$)
$$\begin{aligned} \theta_0 &= 2\pi(1.75) = 3.5 \times 3.14159 = 10.9956\text{ rad} \\[6pt] X' &= \frac{1 \times 40}{2 \times 300} \times 10.9956 = \frac{40}{600} \times 10.9956 = \frac{1}{15} \times 10.9956 \approx \mathbf{0.7330} \end{aligned}$$
🔹 Step 3: Calculate Bessel Function $J_1(X')$ & RF Output Power ($P_{\text{out}}$)

Using small-argument series expansion $J_1(x) \approx \frac{x}{2} - \frac{x^3}{16}$ for $x = 0.7330$:

$$\begin{aligned} J_1(0.7330) &\approx \frac{0.7330}{2} - \frac{(0.7330)^3}{16} = 0.3665 - 0.0246 = \mathbf{0.3419} \\[6pt] P_{\text{out}} &= V_1 I_0 J_1(X') = 40\text{ V} \times 0.020\text{ A} \times 0.3419 = \mathbf{0.2735\text{ W}} \quad (273.5\text{ mW}) \end{aligned}$$
🔹 Step 4: Calculate Electronic Efficiency ($\eta$)
$$\eta = \frac{P_{\text{out}}}{P_{\text{in}}} \times 100\% = \frac{0.2735\text{ W}}{6.0\text{ W}} \times 100\% = \mathbf{4.558\%}$$
🏆 4. Final Boxed Results
(i) Input Power$P_{\text{in}} = 6.0\text{ W}$
(ii) Output Power$P_{\text{out}} = 0.2735\text{ W} = 273.5\text{ mW}$
(iii) Efficiency$\eta = 4.56\%$
🏆 CATEGORY 5: Two-Cavity Klystron Amplifiers Unit 4 • 1 Solved Problem

📌 Problem 5.1: Two-Cavity Klystron Voltage & Power Delivery

Nov/Dec 2022, Q4(b) 6 Marks
SPPU University Examination Question:
"A 2-cavity Klystron amplifier has the following characteristics: Voltage gain $= 15\text{ dB}$, Input power $P_{\text{in}} = 5\text{ mW}$, $R_{\text{sh}}$ of input cavity $= 30\text{ k}\Omega$, $R_{\text{sh}}$ of output cavity $= 40\text{ k}\Omega$, $R_L$ (load impedance) $= 40\text{ k}\Omega$. Determine:
(i) The input r.m.s. voltage;
(ii) The output r.m.s. voltage;
(iii) The power delivered to the load."
📋 1. Given Parameters
Voltage Gain:$\text{Gain} = 15\text{ dB}$
Input Power ($P_{\text{in}}$):$P_{\text{in}} = 5\text{ mW} = 0.005\text{ W}$
Input Shunt Resistance ($R_{\text{sh1}}$):$R_{\text{sh1}} = 30\text{ k}\Omega$
Output Shunt Resistance ($R_{\text{sh2}}$):$R_{\text{sh2}} = 40\text{ k}\Omega$
Load Resistance ($R_L$):$R_L = 40\text{ k}\Omega$
⚡ 3. Mathematical Calculations
🔹 (i) Input RMS Voltage ($V_{\text{in}}$)
$$P_{\text{in}} = \frac{V_{\text{in}}^2}{R_{\text{sh1}}} \implies V_{\text{in}} = \sqrt{P_{\text{in}} \cdot R_{\text{sh1}}} = \sqrt{5 \times 10^{-3} \times 30,000} = \sqrt{150} \approx \mathbf{12.247\text{ V}}$$
🔹 (ii) Output RMS Voltage ($V_{\text{out}}$)
$$\text{Gain}_{\text{dB}} = 20\log_{10}\left(\frac{V_{\text{out}}}{V_{\text{in}}}\right) = 15 \implies \frac{V_{\text{out}}}{V_{\text{in}}} = 10^{15/20} = 10^{0.75} \approx 5.6234$$ $$V_{\text{out}} = 5.6234 \times 12.247\text{ V} \approx \mathbf{68.87\text{ V}}$$
🔹 (iii) Power Delivered to the Load ($P_L$)
$$P_L = \frac{V_{\text{out}}^2}{R_L} = \frac{(68.87)^2}{40,000} = \frac{4743.08}{40,000} \approx \mathbf{0.1186\text{ W}} \quad (118.6\text{ mW})$$
🏆 4. Final Boxed Results
(i) Vin (RMS)$V_{\text{in}} = 12.25\text{ V}$
(ii) Vout (RMS)$V_{\text{out}} = 68.87\text{ V}$
(iii) Power to Load$P_L = 118.6\text{ mW} = 0.119\text{ W}$

📌 Problem 6.1: IMPATT Diode Drift Time and Operating Frequency

Nov/Dec 2022, Q6(b) 6 Marks
SPPU University Examination Question:
"An IMPATT diode has a drift length of $2\ \mu\text{m}$. Determine:
(i) The drift time of the carrier;
(ii) The operating frequency of the diode.
*(Take carrier saturation drift velocity $v_d = 10^7\text{ cm/s} = 10^5\text{ m/s}$ in Silicon)*"
📋 1. Given Parameters

Drift Velocity ($v_d$):$v_d = 10^7\text{ cm/s} = 10^5\text{ m/s}$
⚡ 3. Mathematical Calculations
🔹 (i) Carrier Drift Transit Time ($\tau$)
$$\tau = \frac{L}{v_d} = \frac{2 \times 10^{-6}\text{ m}}{10^5\text{ m/s}} = 2 \times 10^{-11}\text{ s} = \mathbf{20.0\text{ ps}} \quad (\text{picoseconds})$$

🏆 CATEGORY 7: Microwave Measurements & Slotted Line (Double Minimum Method) Unit 6 • 2 Solved Problems

📌 Problem 7.1: Double Minimum Method for High VSWR Calculation

🔥 Repeated 2x (2022, 2025) 6 Marks
SPPU University Examination Question:
"Calculate the SWR of a transmission system operating at $10\text{ GHz}$. Assume $\text{TE}_{10}$ wave transmission inside a rectangular waveguide of dimensions $a = 4\text{ cm}, b = 2.5\text{ cm}$. The distance measured between twice minimum power points is $\Delta x = 1\text{ mm}$ on a slotted line."
📋 1. Given Parameters
Frequency ($f$):$f = 10\text{ GHz} = 10 \times 10^9\text{ Hz}$
Broad Dimension ($a$):$a = 4.0\text{ cm}$
Mode:$\text{TE}_{10} \implies \lambda_c = 2a = 8.0\text{ cm}$
Distance ($\Delta x$):$\Delta x = 1\text{ mm} = 0.1\text{ cm}$
⚡ 3. Mathematical Calculations
🔹 Step 1: Calculate Free-Space Wavelength ($\lambda_0$) and Cut-off Wavelength ($\lambda_c$)
$$\lambda_0 = \frac{c}{f} = \frac{3 \times 10^{10}\text{ cm/s}}{10 \times 10^9\text{ s}^{-1}} = \mathbf{3.0\text{ cm}}, \quad \lambda_c = 2a = 2(4\text{ cm}) = \mathbf{8.0\text{ cm}}$$
🔹 Step 2: Calculate Guide Wavelength ($\lambda_g$)
$$\begin{aligned} \lambda_g &= \frac{\lambda_0}{\sqrt{1 - \left(\frac{\lambda_0}{\lambda_c}\right)^2}} = \frac{3.0}{\sqrt{1 - \left(\frac{3}{8}\right)^2}} = \frac{3.0}{\sqrt{1 - \frac{9}{64}}} = \frac{3.0}{\sqrt{\frac{55}{64}}} = \frac{3.0 \times 8}{\sqrt{55}} \\[6pt] \lambda_g &= \frac{24}{7.4162} \approx \mathbf{3.2362\text{ cm}} \end{aligned}$$
🔹 Step 3: Calculate High VSWR via Double Minimum Formula
$$\text{VSWR} = S \approx \frac{\lambda_g}{\pi \Delta x} = \frac{3.2362\text{ cm}}{\pi \times 0.10\text{ cm}} = \frac{3.2362}{0.314159} \approx \mathbf{10.30}$$
🏆 4. Final Boxed Result
Standing Wave Ratio$\text{SWR} = 10.30$

📌 Problem 7.2: Frequency Measurement from Slotted Line Voltage Nulls

May/Jun 2025, Q7(a) 6 Marks
SPPU University Examination Question:
"A slotted line is used to measure the frequency and it was found that the distance between adjacent nulls is $1.85\text{ cm}$. Given guide dimensions are $3 \times 1.5\text{ cm}$. Calculate the value of frequency."
📋 1. Given Parameters
Adjacent Null Spacing ($d_{\text{null}}$):$d_{\text{null}} = 1.85\text{ cm}$
Waveguide Dimensions:$a = 3.0\text{ cm}, \quad b = 1.5\text{ cm}$
Dominant Mode:$\text{TE}_{10} \implies \lambda_c = 2a = 6.0\text{ cm}$
⚡ 3. Mathematical Calculations
🔹 Step 1: Calculate Guide Wavelength ($\lambda_g$)
$$\frac{\lambda_g}{2} = d_{\text{null}} \implies \lambda_g = 2 \times 1.85\text{ cm} = \mathbf{3.70\text{ cm}}$$
🔹 Step 2: Calculate Free-Space Wavelength ($\lambda_0$)
$$\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{\lambda_0^2} &= \frac{1}{\lambda_g^2} + \frac{1}{\lambda_c^2} \implies \lambda_0 = \frac{\lambda_g \lambda_c}{\sqrt{\lambda_g^2 + \lambda_c^2}} \\[6pt] \lambda_0 &= \frac{3.70 \times 6.0}{\sqrt{(3.70)^2 + (6.0)^2}} = \frac{22.20}{\sqrt{13.69 + 36.00}} = \frac{22.20}{\sqrt{49.69}} = \frac{22.20}{7.0491} \approx \mathbf{3.1493\text{ cm}} \end{aligned}$$
🔹 Step 3: Calculate Operating Microwave Frequency ($f$)
$$f = \frac{c}{\lambda_0} = \frac{3 \times 10^{10}\text{ cm/s}}{3.1493\text{ cm}} \approx 9.5258 \times 10^9\text{ Hz} = \mathbf{9.53\text{ GHz}}$$
🏆 4. Final Boxed Result
Operating Frequency$f = 9.53\text{ GHz}$
🏆 CATEGORY 8: Radar Range Equation & Target Cross-Section Unit 6 • 2 Solved Problems

📌 Problem 8.1: Maximum Radar Detection Range ($R_{\max}$)

🔥 Repeated 2x (2023, 2024) 6 Marks
SPPU University Examination Question:
"Calculate the maximum range of a radar system operating at wavelength $\lambda = 3\text{ cm}$ with a peak pulse power of $600\text{ kW}$. The antenna effective area is $5\text{ m}^2$, minimum detectable signal is $S_{\min} = 10^{-13}\text{ W}$, and target radar cross-sectional area is $\sigma = 20\text{ m}^2$."
📋 1. Given Parameters
Wavelength ($\lambda$):$\lambda = 3\text{ cm} = 0.03\text{ m}$
Peak Power ($P_t$):$P_t = 600\text{ kW} = 6 \times 10^5\text{ W}$
Effective Aperture ($A_e$):$A_e = 5\text{ m}^2$
Radar Cross Section ($\sigma$):$\sigma = 20\text{ m}^2$
Min Detectable Signal ($S_{\min}$):$S_{\min} = 10^{-13}\text{ W}$
📐 2. Key Radar Range Formula
$$R_{\max} = \left[ \frac{P_t \cdot A_e^2 \cdot \sigma}{4\pi \cdot \lambda^2 \cdot S_{\min}} \right]^{1/4}$$
⚡ 3. Mathematical Calculations
🔹 Step 1: Calculate Numerator
$$\text{Num} = (6 \times 10^5) \times (5)^2 \times 20 = 6 \times 10^5 \times 25 \times 20 = 3.0 \times 10^8\text{ W}\cdot\text{m}^4$$
🔹 Step 2: Calculate Denominator
$$\text{Den} = 4\pi \times (0.03)^2 \times 10^{-13} = 4 \times 3.14159265 \times 0.0009 \times 10^{-13} \approx 1.13097 \times 10^{-15}$$
🔹 Step 3: Compute Ratio and Fourth Root
$$\begin{aligned} R_{\max}^4 &= \frac{3.0 \times 10^8}{1.13097 \times 10^{-15}} \approx 2.6526 \times 10^{23}\text{ m}^4 \\[6pt] R_{\max} &= (2.6526 \times 10^{23})^{1/4} = (26.526 \times 10^{22})^{1/4} = 717,860\text{ m} \approx \mathbf{717.86\text{ km}} \end{aligned}$$
🏆 4. Final Boxed Result
Maximum Range$R_{\max} = 717.86\text{ km}$

📌 Problem 8.2: Target Cross-Sectional Area ($\sigma$)

May/Jun 2025, Q8(c) 6 Marks
SPPU University Examination Question:
"A marine radar operating at $10\text{ GHz}$ has a maximum range of $50\text{ km}$ with an antenna gain of $4000$. If the transmitter has a power of $250\text{ kW}$ and minimum detectable signal of $10^{-11}\text{ W}$, determine the cross section of the target the radar can sight."
📋 1. Given Parameters
Frequency ($f$):$f = 10\text{ GHz} \implies \lambda = \frac{c}{f} = 0.03\text{ m}$
Max Range ($R_{\max}$):$R_{\max} = 50\text{ km} = 5 \times 10^4\text{ m}$
Antenna Power Gain ($G$):$G = 4000$
Peak Transmitter Power ($P_t$):$P_t = 250\text{ kW} = 2.5 \times 10^5\text{ W}$
Min Detectable Signal ($S_{\min}$):$S_{\min} = 10^{-11}\text{ W}$
📐 2. Key Radar Formula
$$\sigma = \frac{R_{\max}^4 \cdot (4\pi)^3 \cdot S_{\min}}{P_t \cdot G^2 \cdot \lambda^2}$$
⚡ 3. Mathematical Calculations
🔹 Step 1: Calculate Quantities
$$\begin{aligned} R_{\max}^4 &= (5 \times 10^4)^4 = 6.25 \times 10^{18}\text{ m}^4 \\[6pt] (4\pi)^3 &= (12.56637)^3 \approx 1984.4 \\[6pt] \text{Numerator} &= (6.25 \times 10^{18}) \times 1984.4 \times 10^{-11} \approx 1.24025 \times 10^{11} \end{aligned}$$
🔹 Step 2: Calculate Denominator
$$\text{Denominator} = (2.5 \times 10^5) \times (4000)^2 \times (0.03)^2 = 2.5 \times 10^5 \times (1.6 \times 10^7) \times 0.0009 = 3.60 \times 10^9$$
🔹 Step 3: Compute Target Cross-Section Area ($\sigma$)
$$\sigma = \frac{1.24025 \times 10^{11}}{3.60 \times 10^9} \approx \mathbf{34.45\text{ m}^2}$$
🏆 4. Final Boxed Result
Target Cross-Section Area$\sigma = 34.45\text{ m}^2$
